# CS-250-Software-Development-Lifecycle
SNHU CS-250 Software Development Lifecycle portfolio
Reflection

How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I interpret user needs by first understanding what the user is trying to accomplish and then breaking those needs down into clear requirements that can be developed and tested. Creating user stories helps with this because they describe a feature from the user’s point of view and explain the value that the feature should provide. User stories also give the development team a clearer understanding of what needs to be completed and provide acceptance criteria that can be used to determine whether the feature meets the user’s expectations.

How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

I approach developing programs by breaking larger problems into smaller and more manageable tasks. I believe planning, testing, communication, and receiving feedback throughout development can help produce better software. In my future development work, I hope to incorporate Agile practices such as Sprint Planning, Daily Scrums, Sprint Reviews, Retrospectives, and continuous testing. These practices allow a development team to respond to changes, identify problems earlier, and continuously improve the product instead of waiting until the end of development.

What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member in software development means communicating clearly, being dependable, respecting the ideas and responsibilities of others, and being willing to give and receive feedback. Software development requires collaboration between different roles, including developers, testers, Product Owners, and Scrum Masters. A strong team member completes their responsibilities while also supporting the overall goals of the team. I have learned that communication and collaboration are especially important in Agile because everyone must work together to adapt to changes and deliver software that meets the user’s needs.
